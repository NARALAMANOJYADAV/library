📖 About the Library

Definition

Library science is an interdisciplinary field that applies the practices of management, information technology, and education to libraries — covering the collection, organization, preservation, and dissemination of information resources. Martin Schrettinger coined the discipline in his work (1808–1828). The first American school for library science was founded by Melvil Dewey at Columbia University in 1887.

Education and Training

Academic courses in library science include collection management, information systems, research methods, user studies, information literacy, cataloging, classification, preservation, and reference. The field constantly evolves, incorporating database management, information architecture, and knowledge management.

Types of Librarianship

Public Libraries

Covers cataloging, collection development, information literacy, readers' advisory, and community-centered programming for diverse communities of adults, children, and teens.

School Libraries

Covers library services for children in primary through secondary school, including intellectual freedom, pedagogy, information literacy, and cooperative curriculum building.

Academic Libraries

Covers library services for colleges and universities — copyright, digital libraries, academic freedom, open access to scholarly works, and subject-specific reference.

Preservation

Preservation librarians manage activities to maintain access to books, manuscripts, archival materials, and other resources — including binding, conservation, digital reformatting, and environmental monitoring.

History

Libraries have existed for many centuries. The earliest text on library operations, Advice on Establishing a Library, was published in 1627 by Gabriel Naudé. In the 19th century, tools for bibliographic control of printed knowledge were developed. Melvil Dewey's decimal classification (1876) and the opening of the first American library school in 1887 marked major milestones.

21st Century

The digital age transformed information access. Mobile devices, high-speed networks, and cloud computing have deeply impacted library science. All catalogs, databases, and a growing number of books are now available online, with open access journals and platforms like Wikipedia fundamentally changing how information is accessed.
